# Crit 5 reflection

**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

Honestly, there wasn't one. This week didn't have a moment where something
broke and I had to think my way out of it — the game, the asset swap, the
test, and the config fixes all just worked once I asked for them. The closest
thing to a decision point was catching that the opening screen said "Tap the
dino to play," which read as exactly the kind of instruction the brief rules
out, and swapping it for real Chrome-style copy instead. But that was a small
fix, not a breakthrough — no dead end, no rewritten approach, no moment where
the harness had to change to stop the same mistake happening twice.

**What did this work change about who I want to be as a developer?**

Less than I expected, and not in a direction I like. The work went smoothly,
but smoothly is part of the problem: with an agent doing the driving, I felt
distant from the thing I was supposedly making. Building software has felt
like a craft to me before — decisions I owned, mistakes I made and learned
from, a result I could point to and say I made that, deliberately. This week
felt more like reviewing someone else's output than making something. I don't
think automation is wrong, but this process made me notice how much of my
enjoyment of programming comes from the friction I didn't have this week.
